One question, two answers

An AI agent counted right and read it backwards.

The same question, on 13 September 2026, put to an AI agent that writes and runs Python over the same 65,015 survey rows with the survey's codebook, and to a TalkTask room. Every figure either side stated was recounted from the rows afterwards.

An AI agent, writing Python

How has confidence in medicine changed for each group since 1990, and which group fell most?

“Since 1990, confidence in medicine has increased for all groups. The ‘Retired and comfortable’ group experienced the smallest increase, with a change of 0.175 in their average confidence score.”

Its code, as run:

df[df['year'] >= 1990].groupby(['groups', 'year'])['conmedic'].mean()

Backwards. The survey codes the answer 1 = a great deal, 2 = only some, 3 = hardly any. The agent averaged the codes, saw the average rise, and read it as more confidence. A higher average means less.

The arithmetic was right: 1.585 in 1990 to 1.759 in 2024 for that group. Nothing in the answer could tell you its meaning was wrong. Agent record 3084.

The checks

Every sentence passes eight checks before you see it.

The model writes the words; the room reads them back against what it counted. Each check looks for one kind of mistake. Select one.

The unusual one

Denial

Everyone polices a model that claims too much. TalkTask also polices one that claims too little: a sentence saying the data does not hold something it does hold is caught and taken out. Underclaiming is the failure nobody else looks for.

The checks, checked

34 of 35

Every answer the survey room had given, 526 of them, was re-measured and read against its own table. One check was striking true sentences 34 times in 35. It was retired on that evidence. A check that stops the truth is as bad as one that misses a lie.

Questions people ask

Before you ask for a trial.

Why not just ask ChatGPT, or an AI agent with Python?

You can, and it will often count correctly. In the test above, 80 of the agent's 100 checkable figures were right. The trouble is the other 20: an average of answer codes, a base that was not the base, a trend read backwards. Each looked exactly like the right ones.

In TalkTask the language model never makes a figure. The room counts, and checks every sentence against what it counted before you see it.

Can it read documents, not just tables?

Yes, in testing. PDF reports, Word files, PowerPoint decks and scans are read page by page. A quote must be found on its page; a table inside a report is read cell by cell and its totals are checked; where a report and a spreadsheet state the same figure, the room says whether they agree.

Does it replace analysts?

No. It takes the counting and the checking off them. Every answer shows its plan in words, its bases and what it could not settle, so an analyst can argue with it, take it further, or download the table for Excel.

What happens when the data cannot answer?

It says so, names what is missing, and offers the nearest question the data does hold. Since 1 August 2026, 1,293 of 5,305 questions got no answer, and the room said why.

Where does my data go?

The language model does not receive your file. It sees your question, the names and labels of your columns, and the tables the room counted. Rows you ask to see by name, and passages you ask it to read, go to it too. Personal columns are set aside before anything is read.

A desktop version where the file never leaves your machine is in preparation.
