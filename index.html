What it solves

TalkTask answers questions about the data you already hold, in plain words. Every figure is counted by the engine and carries its receipt. Pick a problem, then switch between today and with TalkTask.

What it can do

One table of 32 operations. The language model reads your question and picks from it; the room checks the choice against your data and does the arithmetic. Select any operation to see a real question that used it.

Your sources, in minutes

Files, folders and databases take the same path. Every column is classified from its own rows, personal data is set aside, and every figure is recounted before the room opens. If the recount fails, the room does not open. Pick a source to replay a real intake from the record.

    The checks

    A language model writes the sentences. Before you see them, the room checks each one against what it counted. A sentence that fails does not reach you.

    Asked of a public file of 4,803 films, 28 September 2026

    what measures do you have to measure how the movie did?

    The model's draft: The room does not hold direct measures of movie performance such as box office revenue or audience ratings. However, it can analyze related factors such as production budget and genre…

    Wrong. The file holds box office revenue in dollars.

    The room's check, as recorded: claimed not to hold what this analysis holds … this analysis holds revenue ("box office revenue in dollars")

    The person saw: “I will not give you that answer, because it did not pass the checks”, with the reasons. Room record 3876.

    Asked of the General Social Survey, 8 October 2026

    what are the main differences between people following BASEBALL in US and SOCCER in US

    • 'following baseball in the US' is not in this data
    • 'following soccer in the US' is not in this data

    Its plan then said: “The survey holds nothing about following baseball or soccer, so the nearest measurable thing is every question ranked by the largest difference between the survey's own groups.” Room record 4625.
