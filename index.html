Asked of the General Social Survey, 1972 to 2024

Are americans more or less happy as compared to the past

The plan, in words: how the share giving each answer to general happiness has moved from the first decade to the last.

  • 'pretty happy': in 1970s to in 2020s, 4.1 points up.
  • 'very happy': in 1970s to in 2020s, 14.1 points down.
  • 'not too happy': in 1970s to in 2020s, 10 points up.

The middle of the distribution barely moved: 'pretty happy' is the median answer in every decade. The change is at the two ends, and they moved in opposite directions. That is a hollowing of the top, not a slide of the whole distribution.

Since 1 August 2026 the rooms have been asked 5,305 questions, by the founder, by testers and by an outside benchmark. In 1,293 of them the room gave no answer, and said so.

The model plans and writes. The room counts and checks.

The language model never produces a figure. It proposes what to measure and puts the results into words. Everything in between is ordinary arithmetic on your rows.

  1. The modelThe room
  2. FirstPlansReads your question and writes a plan: which question in the data, which people, which breakdown.
  3. ThenChecks the planEach step is tested against what your file holds. Nothing is guessed. A step that cannot run is named.
  4. ThenCountsTables, trends, gaps and tests, computed from the rows. Every cell keeps its base.
  5. ThenChecks the resultDoes what was counted answer what you asked? If not, the plan goes back once with the reason.
  6. ThenWritesPuts the counted results into plain sentences.
  7. ThenChecks the wordsEvery figure, base and quote is matched to the count. A sentence that fails is removed.
  8. LastYou seeThe answer, the plan in words, the tables and a receipt for every figure. Or a plain reason why not.

Bring the data you already have.

Each file is read once. Every column is classified from its own rows, personal data is set aside, and the room recounts everything before it opens. These have gone through it:

  • A 52-year social survey: 75,699 respondents, 1972 to 2024
  • A face-to-face study in ten Indian cities: 2,623 interviews
  • A brand tracker in two waves: 5,832 respondents
  • 33,489 app reviews and comments over ten years
  • 5,723,532 bike-share trips in twelve monthly files
  • A year of YouTube trending videos: 208,686 records
  • 4,803 films with budgets, revenue and genres
  • A folder of linked tables on US school funding and test scores

It reads CSV, Excel, SPSS, Stata, Parquet, JSON and Triple-S files, zipped folders of them, and Postgres and BigQuery tables. Reading reports, decks and Word files page by page is in testing.
